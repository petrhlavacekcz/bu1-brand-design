# BU1 v2 — Brand Direction

Status: **draft / canonical within v2**

This document defines the visual and creative direction of the BU1 v2 system. It is intentionally broader than a storefront style guide: the same principles must scale from web and social to paid media, print, packaging, retail, products and motion.

## North star

> **BU1 combines oversized product-led composition, powerful condensed typography, restrained monochrome space and precise goalkeeper-specific proof.**

The result should feel authored, specialist and premium — never like a generic sports template or marketplace asset.

## 1. Brand character

BU1 v2 should feel:

- specialist rather than mass-market,
- confident rather than loud,
- technical rather than jargon-heavy,
- athletic rather than aggressive,
- editorial rather than templated,
- premium rather than luxurious for its own sake,
- direct rather than over-written.

The visual world sits between **performance sport, technical product design and fashion-editorial commerce**.

## 2. The core equation

```text
EXACT PRODUCT
+ BOLD CONDENSED TYPOGRAPHY
+ NEGATIVE SPACE
+ ASYMMETRICAL TENSION
+ PRECISE PRODUCT PROOF
= BU1 V2
```

Not every asset needs every ingredient at maximum intensity, but the hierarchy must remain recognizable.

## 3. Scene for one hero

The interface, ad, poster, package or product graphic is a stage.

The hierarchy is:

1. **hero product or hero athlete moment**
2. **hero typography**
3. **technical proof**
4. **commerce action**
5. **utilities / metadata**

This principle does **not** mean empty design. It means intentional hierarchy.

## 4. Product fidelity is non-negotiable

For sellable BU1 products:

- preserve the exact model,
- preserve the exact colorway,
- preserve real patterns and materials,
- preserve official marks,
- preserve silhouette and proportions,
- never invent product technology or construction,
- never redraw the product only because the model can generate something visually similar.

Preferred production order:

1. exact photography / exact product asset,
2. exact-product composite,
3. exact product inside a generated environment,
4. full AI only when no identifiable sellable product needs to remain exact.

## 5. Logo fidelity is non-negotiable

- Use official BU1 logo assets only.
- Do not generate or approximate the logo.
- Do not replace it with an invented mascot, symbol or wordmark.
- Omit a standalone logo rather than showing a wrong one.

The product itself may already carry enough brand recognition in some social creative.

## 6. Product-led color

BU1 v2 does not force one accent color onto every product.

The **product is usually the primary color event**.

Example:

- MiKos → cyan/blue details lead the visual accent,
- another model may naturally lead with red, green, orange or neutral tones.

The surrounding composition should normally stay restrained enough to let the product own its color.

## 7. Hot Rose is semantic

Hot Rose is a BU1 signal, not decoration.

Good uses:

- `NOVINKA`
- `DROP`
- `LIMITED`
- `VÝPRODEJ`
- urgency / active promotional state
- selected active UI state

Bad uses:

- filling empty space,
- making every asset look more branded,
- competing with the product color,
- becoming a large decorative background without a semantic reason.

## 8. Two expression modes

### Light Editorial

Default for:

- storefront,
- launch heroes,
- social feed,
- premium print,
- packaging,
- product launch sheets.

Characteristics:

- white / off-white / warm neutral foundation,
- black display typography,
- exact product as the color event,
- generous negative space,
- subtle technical annotation,
- controlled asymmetry.

### Dark Impact

Preferred selectively for:

- Stories / Reels covers,
- high-attention paid social,
- event posters,
- launch teasers,
- retail impact moments.

Characteristics:

- black / deep charcoal foundation,
- white display typography,
- stronger crop and contrast,
- restrained texture / grain / streaks if useful,
- product color remains the main accent,
- Hot Rose stays semantic.

Dark Impact must still feel editorial. It must not collapse into generic gaming, neon or action-sports graphics.

## 9. Typography direction

### Display

The v2 editorial display direction is **Anton-style condensed uppercase typography**. Use the exact approved storefront font implementation when available.

Rules:

- uppercase by default for hero statements,
- very large scale is encouraged,
- short phrases outperform long copy,
- punctuation — especially the full stop — can be used as a graphic device,
- keep line-height tight but readable,
- do not fake the style with unrelated condensed fonts in final brand work.

Examples:

- `MIKOS.`
- `LEHKOST. CIT. JISTOTA.`
- `PŘIROZENÝ POCIT.`
- `TVÉ ÚZEMÍ.`

### UI / supporting text

Use the approved neutral sans-serif from the storefront system (Nunito Sans in the current redesign direction) for:

- navigation,
- body copy,
- microcopy,
- technical proof,
- product facts,
- labels where display type would overpower the hierarchy.

## 10. Copy behavior

Copy should be:

- short,
- concrete,
- goalkeeper-specific,
- factual,
- confident,
- easy to read in one glance.

Preferred hierarchy:

```text
semantic label
hero product / claim
support line
proof
price / offer
CTA
```

Not every asset needs every layer.

Avoid:

- revolutionary,
- ultimate,
- game-changing,
- premium quality,
- best ever,
- vague emotional filler,

unless the statement is genuinely proven and useful.

## 11. Composition grammar

BU1 v2 prefers authored composition over balanced templates.

Strong mechanisms include:

### Oversized product crop

The product may occupy 40–75% of the canvas and may deliberately leave the frame.

### Scale contrast

One very large object or word is paired with very small technical information.

### Ghost typography

Very large low-contrast product / range text may sit behind the product as a structural layer.

### Technical annotation

Small lines, dots, labels, price blocks or proof points can behave like product-design annotations.

### Directional line

A thin line / arrow may create movement or connect information to the hero product.

### Macro proof

A grip, material, construction or fit detail may replace the whole-product shot when the proof itself is the story.

### Athlete × product

Use real athlete interaction when collaboration, fit, confidence or action is the story. The product must remain recognizable and accurate.

## 12. Inspiration and originality

Inspia, campaign archives, photography, fashion/editorial work and other references are valid **art-direction research sources**.

Extract:

- composition,
- crop,
- lighting,
- scale,
- camera position,
- negative space,
- shot order,
- motion grammar,
- material treatment,
- visual hook.

Do not copy:

- another brand identity,
- another logo,
- another slogan,
- a distinctive finished layout one-to-one,
- product claims that do not belong to BU1.

The durable asset is the **visual mechanism**, not the external reference itself.

## 13. What BU1 v2 must not become

Avoid:

- generic stadium backgrounds,
- fake crowds,
- random lens flares,
- gratuitous gradients,
- neon gaming aesthetics,
- 3–5 competing typefaces,
- generic e-commerce cards in hero creative,
- multiple badges fighting for attention,
- invented product details,
- invented brand marks,
- decorative color with no hierarchy,
- over-written layouts.

## 14. Recognition test

An asset passes the BU1 v2 direction if the answer is yes to most of these questions:

- Is there one clear visual hero?
- Is the sellable product exact?
- Does the product own the color?
- Is typography bold enough to feel authored?
- Is there controlled negative space?
- Is the layout asymmetric or intentionally tensioned?
- Is technical proof concise and real?
- Is Hot Rose semantic?
- Does the asset work without visual clutter?
- Would it still feel like BU1 if the standalone logo disappeared?
