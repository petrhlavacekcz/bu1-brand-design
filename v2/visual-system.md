# BU1 v2 — Visual System

Status: **draft**

## 1. Visual formula

The BU1 v2 visual system is built from five recurring ingredients:

1. oversized exact product,
2. bold condensed display typography,
3. controlled negative space,
4. restrained monochrome foundation,
5. concise technical proof.

The target is **editorial performance**, not decorative minimalism.

## 2. Composition

### Preferred behavior

- Product commonly occupies 40–70% of the canvas.
- Product may crop beyond one or more edges.
- Slight rotation or diagonal entry is encouraged when it improves tension.
- Layouts should feel asymmetric but deliberate.
- Large empty areas are allowed when they increase product or headline impact.
- Typography and product should usually be the two dominant masses.

### Avoid

- centered catalogue layouts by default,
- product inside unnecessary cards,
- symmetrical product + text halves with no tension,
- decorative UI boxes that compete with the product,
- multiple equal-weight messages.

## 3. Typography

### Display

Working v2 direction: **Anton** for major editorial display usage unless/until a later v2 typography decision replaces it.

Use for:

- hero headlines,
- campaign names,
- product names,
- large prices where appropriate,
- poster-scale statements.

Behavior:

- uppercase by default,
- short statements,
- strong scale,
- deliberate line breaks,
- tight but readable tracking,
- high contrast against background.

The font is not enough on its own. Correct scale, line height, whitespace and interaction with product imagery are part of the system.

### UI / body

Working v2 direction: **Nunito Sans** for interface, body, utilities and supporting copy.

Use for:

- navigation,
- buttons,
- technical information,
- body text,
- labels,
- filters,
- product metadata.

### Hierarchy

Typical order:

1. display headline,
2. hero product,
3. support line or price,
4. technical proof,
5. CTA,
6. utility information.

Do not give all text the same weight.

## 4. Color

### Foundation

The default canvas is deliberately restrained:

- white,
- off-white / warm light gray,
- near-black / Onyx,
- subtle neutral dividers and ghost text.

### Product accent

A product’s real color may become the dominant campaign accent. This is encouraged when it strengthens the product rather than the surrounding UI.

### Hot Rose

Hot Rose remains a BU1 semantic accent.

Use sparingly for meaningful state labels such as:

- NOVINKA,
- VÝPRODEJ,
- LIMITED,
- DROP,
- active promotion.

A layout should still work if Hot Rose is removed. It must not be the structure holding the design together.

## 5. Two official expression modes

### Light Editorial

Best for:

- homepage heroes,
- category banners,
- product launches,
- social feed,
- print,
- packaging.

Characteristics:

- white / warm-white field,
- black display type,
- exact product color stands out,
- quiet line work,
- small semantic accents,
- high negative space.

### Dark Impact

Best for:

- stories,
- Reels covers,
- high-attention paid social,
- posters,
- launch moments,
- event and retail display.

Characteristics:

- deep black / charcoal field,
- white display type,
- exact product color remains vivid,
- stronger crop and contrast,
- restrained texture or halftone allowed,
- more motion energy while retaining discipline.

Dark Impact is not permission for generic sports effects. Avoid fake stadiums, random neon, excessive particles and template-like speed lines unless the creative concept truly requires them.

## 6. Reusable visual mechanisms

These are valid compositional patterns, not fixed templates:

### Monumental Product

An oversized product becomes the dominant object and may crop beyond the frame.

### Type × Product

Large display typography and the product overlap or visually compete at similar scale.

### Ghost Word

Very large low-contrast typography sits behind product or content as a structural layer.

### Technical Annotation

Small precise information blocks, rules, dots, arrows or product labels contrast with the oversized hero elements.

### Scale Contrast

Tiny copy / large product / giant headline creates hierarchy through extreme scale differences.

### Editorial Crop

The product enters or exits the frame deliberately rather than being fully contained.

### Dark Product Portrait

Single product against a dark field with minimal copy and controlled directional light.

## 7. Core components

Define and reuse, without turning them into rigid templates:

- semantic label,
- hero headline,
- support line,
- price block,
- CTA button / editorial text link,
- technical proof line,
- thin directional rule / arrow,
- ghost typography,
- exact product cutout,
- exact logo mark,
- desktop header / navigation,
- light and dark surface treatments.

## 8. Texture and effects

Allowed, sparingly:

- subtle halftone,
- faint grain,
- low-contrast ghost lettering,
- restrained editorial rules / lines,
- realistic product shadow,
- subtle motion streaks only for dynamic concepts.

Avoid:

- gradients as decoration,
- generic glow,
- fake lens flares,
- random 3D shapes,
- decorative cyan lines everywhere,
- excessive stickers or badges.

## 9. Photography and product imagery

### Product photography

Preferred:

- exact packshot cutouts,
- macro material detail,
- authentic athlete usage,
- controlled studio light,
- strong crop,
- material texture visible,
- product shape readable at a glance.

### Lifestyle

Use real goalkeeping situations where possible. The image should communicate a useful product or emotional truth, not simply prove that a goalkeeper exists in the frame.

### AI-generated environment

Allowed when useful, but the real product should remain exact and clearly composited. AI must not silently replace the product with an approximation.

## 10. Do / Don't

### Do

- make the product huge,
- use short typography confidently,
- allow negative space,
- use product color naturally,
- use exact brand and product assets,
- build hierarchy with scale,
- use micro-details for credibility.

### Don't

- force pink into every design,
- shrink the product to make room for copy,
- add text because space exists,
- regenerate logos,
- fabricate product features,
- use generic ecommerce visual templates,
- mistake minimalism for emptiness.
