# BU1 v2 — Channel Playbooks

Status: **draft**

The same BU1 v2 identity should adapt to context rather than forcing one layout everywhere.

## 1. Web / storefront

Goal: premium editorial commerce.

### Use

- exact product imagery at dominant scale,
- clean monochrome header and navigation,
- short hero copy,
- clear product name, price and CTA,
- restrained technical proof,
- generous whitespace,
- Light Editorial mode by default.

### Hero structure

A typical hero may contain:

- semantic label,
- product / campaign name,
- short support line,
- price,
- one proof line,
- CTA,
- oversized product.

### Avoid

- dense promo mosaics,
- multiple competing cards in the hero,
- unnecessary badges,
- long paragraphs,
- generic marketplace behavior.

## 2. Social feed

Goal: stop the scroll while remaining unmistakably BU1.

### Preferred formats

- 4:5 for feed,
- 1:1 when required by placement or reuse.

### Rules

- fewer words than web,
- stronger crop,
- headline readable in under one second,
- product must remain recognizable at mobile size,
- visual mechanism may be more experimental,
- CTA can be omitted when platform UI already provides it.

### Recommended creative families

- oversized product + short claim,
- ghost typography + exact product,
- macro material / grip detail,
- athlete + product relationship,
- technical proof poster,
- dark product portrait.

## 3. Stories / vertical social

Goal: immediate mobile impact.

### Rules

- design for 9:16 from the start,
- keep key text inside safe zones,
- one dominant product or one dominant action,
- use Dark Impact more often than on feed,
- use a clear hierarchy: label → headline → product → price / CTA,
- avoid filling the lower UI-safe area with important detail.

### Good use cases

- product launch,
- new colorway,
- limited drop,
- athlete collaboration,
- short technical proof,
- campaign teaser.

## 4. Paid social / PPC

Goal: attention + clear proposition + measurable conversion hypothesis.

### Rules

- one product,
- one primary message,
- one proof point,
- one decision-driving element: price, benefit, offer or CTA,
- no decorative complexity that does not support the test.

### Test variables

Test one major variable at a time where practical:

- headline,
- visual mechanism,
- product crop,
- background mode,
- proof point,
- offer / price framing,
- athlete vs product-only.

Creative systems should preserve concept IDs and learn from results rather than producing disconnected assets.

## 5. Google Display / PMax asset families

Goal: maintain brand recognition across many crops and sizes.

### Build families, not one master image

Prepare separately:

- clean product crop,
- product + headline,
- product + price,
- product + proof,
- dark impact alternative.

Do not depend on tiny embedded text surviving automated crops.

## 6. Print

Goal: physical presence and premium restraint.

### Rules

- exploit physical scale,
- allow generous margins and whitespace,
- use one dominant statement,
- preserve excellent image resolution,
- technical microcopy can be used as editorial annotation,
- paper, finish and print quality are part of the visual system.

### Good applications

- A3/A2 posters,
- retail signage,
- athlete cards,
- event materials,
- product launch sheets,
- B2B showroom material.

Avoid turning print into a dense sales leaflet unless the format explicitly requires a catalogue.

## 7. Packaging

Goal: strong shelf / unboxing identity without visual noise.

### Front-facing principle

Use:

- strong product or model name,
- restrained BU1 branding,
- monochrome base,
- product-led accent,
- minimal semantic labels.

### Information hierarchy

Front:

- brand,
- model / range,
- one relevant visual signal.

Back / side:

- technical specifications,
- care,
- sizing,
- legal / material information,
- QR / support where useful.

The package should not reproduce a web PDP on cardboard.

## 8. Product graphics

Goal: make the product itself recognizable as BU1 without decorating it for decoration’s sake.

### Rules

- function first,
- graphics should respect panel construction and materials,
- accent color belongs to the product concept,
- do not force Hot Rose onto all products,
- maintain a clear BU1 mark system,
- avoid visual noise that makes the glove harder to read from distance.

Campaign graphics may be louder than permanent product graphics.

## 9. Retail / POS

Goal: fast recognition in a physical environment.

Use:

- oversized product image,
- one big headline,
- strong price or key proof where relevant,
- limited copy,
- consistent shelf / wall families.

Retail can use Dark Impact for high-attention moments but should not become visually disconnected from packaging and web.

## 10. Email

Goal: make campaign emails feel like BU1 rather than generic CRM templates.

Use:

- strong opening image,
- short headline,
- one primary CTA,
- product-led color,
- compact product proof.

Avoid turning the email header into a miniature homepage.

## 11. Video / motion

Goal: translate the same editorial system into time.

### Inspiration workflow

Sources such as Inspia can be used to identify:

- opening hook,
- shot order,
- macro vs wide rhythm,
- camera movement,
- reveal mechanics,
- freeze frames,
- product hero ending,
- lighting references.

### Example launch grammar

1. macro material detail,
2. preparation / strap / fit,
3. athlete action,
4. save / grip moment,
5. product freeze or hero shot,
6. short BU1 end frame.

### Rules

- exact product remains exact,
- real footage is preferred when product detail is critical,
- AI may generate atmosphere, transitions or non-product scenes when useful,
- motion design should inherit the same type hierarchy and restrained color behavior as static creative.

## 12. Cross-channel consistency checklist

Before publishing any asset, confirm:

- Is the product exact?
- Is the logo exact?
- Is there one clear visual hero?
- Is the headline short enough?
- Is color coming mainly from the product?
- Is Hot Rose semantic rather than decorative?
- Is the layout authored rather than templated?
- Does the channel need all the information currently shown?
- Does the asset still feel like BU1 without relying on the logo alone?
